01 Project brief

A maze that shifts beneath your feet

Whispers of Labyrinth is a modular strategy board game rooted in the Greek myth of the Labyrinth of Knossos and the Minotaur. Players become hooded archetypes, move through shifting hexagonal corridors to gather Echo Shards and Relics, and race to escape before the labyrinth collapses.

The brief was to design the complete game, from its story and rules to the characters, cards, tiles, 3D-printed tokens, rulebook, packaging and merchandise, with every piece grounded in Minoan culture.

The game story

“To escape is victory. To claim the relics is glory. But to linger too long is to become part of its echo.”

Ariadne's thread · scroll to follow it to the heart

Long ago, before kingdoms rose and fell, a labyrinth was said to be built deeper than the roots of the earth. Some whispered that Daedalus carved it after Knossos: a maze woven not only of stone, but of shifting echoes and living corridors. Others believed it holds the trapped soul of the Minotaur at its heart.

Within its honeycomb walls lie relics and shards of power left behind by those who entered and never returned. Every chamber has a will of its own: corridors stretch, walls close, and the faintest whisper can lead you forward or back to where you began.

You are one of the chosen few who dared to step inside, cloaked in shadow and armed only with your wit and the strength of your archetype. The labyrinth tests more than pathfinding. It tests patience, cunning, courage and sacrifice.

Step 04 · Game logic development

The rules of the maze

Players2–6 Playtime45–75 min Age10+ TypeModular

Setup

  1. Build the board from hex tiles around the central Echo Chamber.
  2. Place Path, Echo Shard, Relic and Echo Trigger tiles at random.
  3. Deal each player a secret Quest Card and a Character Card.

Components

Tiles
30 Path · 7 Quest · 6 Echo Shard · 4 Relic · 10 Echo Trigger · 4 Wall
Cards
30 Quest · 6 Character · 12 Chamber
Coins
80 × 3 pts · 60 × 6 pts · 30 × 9 pts
Dice
1 normal die · 1 Echo Trigger die

On your turn

  • Make one move along the corridor.
  • Quest tile: draw a quest card and resolve it.
  • Echo Shard tile: collect shards.
  • Echo Trigger tile: roll the dice.
  • Relic tile: collect points, and draw a quest card if needed.
  • Near the Echo Chamber, roll: under 3 moves you forward, over 3 moves you back.

When things go wrong

Blocked
If an opponent takes the relic or shard you were heading for, that tile is removed and replaced with a Wall tile.
Stuck
With no path forward, pay shards for a Phantom Pass, wait three turns, or draw a quest card.
Stalemate
After a set number of rounds the labyrinth collapses, and the player with the most points wins.

Winning

  • The player with the most shards and points wins.
  • When the first player exits, the maze collapses and the highest score wins.
  • On a tie, whoever was closest to the exit first wins.

Element of surprise: the Minotaur card

Hidden in the Echo Chamber deck is a single fateful card. Whoever draws it must keep it secret, because revealing it breaks the labyrinth's ancient code. The bearer has to be the first to escape. If someone else gets out first, the Minotaur's wrath drags that player back to the Echo Chamber and strips them of 3 Echo Shards. If the bearer escapes first, they conquer the beast within and take 3 Echo Shards as tribute.

Penalties

Quest
Lose the points the card would have awarded.
Chamber
Lose one shard.
Echo die
Lose six points.
Steps 13–14 · Game testing & reworking the logic

Two rounds at the table, seven fixes

A paper prototype went in front of real players twice. Each round exposed friction that wasn't visible on paper, and every fix went back into the rules before the final components were designed.

Playtest 01

  1. Counting points was difficult.
    →
    Added numbered point tokens.
  2. It was hard to track entry points into the chamber.
    →
    Limited the Echo Chamber to two entries: one at the top, one at the bottom.
  3. Echo Trigger tasks were hard to carry out.
    →
    Removed the clockwise and anticlockwise faces from the die.
  4. Chamber tasks were hard to carry out.
    →
    Rewrote some Echo Chamber cards so the task must be completed in that same round.

Playtest 02

  1. Task cards were taken for granted.
    →
    Added penalties for tasks that aren't completed.
  2. Revealing tasks caused conflict between players.
    →
    Players flip a card only once it's complete, and claim the reward when they flip it.
  3. Keeping track of the rules was hard work.
    →
    Added a small instruction card for each player.
Step 12 · Tokens

From Blender to the tabletop

The player token is a hooded, faceless silhouette that signals mystery and authority at a glance. It was sculpted in Blender, sliced and 3D-printed, then tested at its real 4 cm tabletop scale.

The clean form reads clearly at 4 cm, and its neutral design lets players project their own identity onto it. It feels less like a pawn and more like a presence within the labyrinth.

03 Research & history

Borrowed from the gods

Every tile, card and symbol in the game traces back to the myths and artefacts of Minoan Crete.

Cultural reference · Minotaur

The Labyrinth of Knossos

King Minos had Daedalus build a maze beneath his palace at Knossos to imprison the Minotaur, the half-bull son of his wife Pasiphaë, born of Poseidon's curse. Athens sent young tributes inside until Theseus, guided by Ariadne's thread, slew the beast and found his way out.

Cultural reference · Path tiles

Zeus vs Poseidon

Poseidon's wounded pride created the Minotaur; Zeus, seeking balance, favoured those who set out to destroy it. Their rivalry runs through the board: Zeus's lightning cracks every path tile, and Poseidon's trident marks the task tile.

From artefact to component

Each reference became a piece of the game

The labrys · double-sided axe

Carved across the palace at Knossos, the double axe stood for divine authority, balance and protection. "Labyrinth" is believed to mean "house of the double axe".

The meander

Found on Cretan seals, coins and ritual objects, the endless winding line signified order, protection and sacred journeying rather than confusion.

Rulebook · ideation & print

Rules you unfold

The rulebook uses a maze fold. Players open it layer by layer, just as they uncover paths and secrets on the board. Each fold reveals one stage of the rules, so complex mechanics arrive one at a time instead of all at once. The rulebook feels like part of the game world, not a separate manual.

05 Learnings

What the labyrinth taught me

  1. Playtesting rewrites the rules

    Two rounds at the table surfaced problems I couldn't see on paper: counting, chamber entries, ignored tasks. Almost every fix made the game simpler, not bigger.

  2. Myth makes a system feel inevitable

    Rooting every tile, card and symbol in a real Minoan reference, from the labrys and meander to the coins and trident, gave the visual language one consistent logic and a story behind each piece.

  3. Format can carry meaning

    The maze-fold rulebook showed that how information is revealed is part of the experience. Unfolding the rules mirrors exploring the maze.

  4. Design for the hand, not the screen

    3D printing a 4 cm token and building a die-cut box meant designing for physical scale, legibility at arm's length, and how pieces feel on the table.
