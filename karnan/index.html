Project Brief

Pair a filmmaker with an artist who sees the world the way they do, then rebuild one film’s posters in that artist’s hand.

I paired Mari Selvaraj’s Karnan with the linocuts of Chittaprosad Bhattacharya. Both refuse glamour and polish, and both put the oppressed at the centre of the frame instead of the background.

The work became a new storyline for the campaign, a hand-carved title and a series of four posters. The series moves from a pre-title teaser to the final release and reveals the hero slowly, letting symbols build the tension before he appears.

Title Design

Sketches

The title takes its cue from Chittaprosad Bhattacharya, whose bold, raw and unpolished prints spoke for the struggles of ordinary people. Like his woodcuts and linocuts, the lettering is lino-cut inspired, with rough, uneven textures that look carved by hand and give it a direct, forceful presence.

An embossed, stone-like finish makes it feel monumental, suggesting permanence and resilience. Minimal but powerful, it carries the essence of Karnan.

03

Research

Director · Artist · Film · Storyline · Fusion

The director

Mari Selvaraj

Tamil filmmaker and writer

A critically acclaimed filmmaker whose socially charged films, rooted in personal and rural experience, confront caste, marginalisation and resilience with raw emotional depth.

Storytelling

A clear split between everyday life and outcry, with tension that builds slowly. Characters begin passive and grow active. Animal metaphors and cultural rituals run through the films.

Ideology

Caste discrimination is shown in systemic, psychological terms. His protagonists often come from Dalit or lower-caste communities who rise against injustice.

Visual tone

Naturalistic, high-contrast imagery; wide-angle and handheld shots; minimal colour grading and a sun-bleached palette.

Vision

Dalit, rural and working-class people appear as full protagonists with agency and dignity, never as side characters. Defiance is shown as a personal and collective need for dignity. Music, folk art, landscape and dialect record village culture without romanticising poverty.

Publicity designer · Kabilan Chelliah

His posters are raw and real: cracked walls, dusty land, sunburnt skies, minimal text and maximum image. Typography stays restrained and functional, and his earthy ochre, rust, blood red, ash grey and muted green come from the soil and struggles of the land.

The artist

Chittaprosad Bhattacharya

1915–1978 · Linocut, woodcut, ink

A revolutionary Indian artist who rejected elite traditions for accessible mediums, linocuts, woodcuts and sketches, to document famine, poverty and exploitation under colonial and feudal rule.

Storytelling

His work reads like a report or a fable. Linocuts often carry captions; pages of Hungry Bengal had handwritten notes beside each drawing. In his famine notebooks he named each peasant and recorded their circumstances. Elsewhere he used allegory, visual metaphor and cultural symbols.

Ideology

Part of India’s progressive political art: socialist realism with expressionist and folk-inspired roots.

Visual tone

Pen-and-ink drawings and black-and-white prints with bold contours and high contrast. His empathy shows famine victims, peasants and workers as people crushed by systemic violence, never faceless statistics.

Why this pair

Each character stands for a people, not just one person.

Both reject glamour and polish and insist the oppressed are the protagonists. Their images are scarred, raw, folk, mythic and earthy, made to show pain and dignity together. Their art is resistance that listens to silenced voices, compressing collective suffering and defiance into symbols.

Film study

Karnan, 2021

Socio-political drama · Tamil

Set in a Tamil Nadu village crushed by caste discrimination, Karnan follows a brave young man from a marginalised community who rises against the injustice and humiliation his people face, down to being denied something as basic as a bus stop.

Design language

Raw and earthy, rooted in rural Tamil Nadu. No glossy visuals: dust, mud, cracked walls and worn fabric make it authentic, and the production design reflects the harsh land and the community’s reality.

Colour palette

Ochre and sandy brown: heat, drought, hardship
Deep red and maroon: resistance, blood, rebellion
Dusty green and faded blue: keep it grounded and real

Symbolism

  • Tied donkey: helplessness, a voice suppressed
  • Broken statues and idols: shattered identity, cultural erasure
  • The sword: justice and uprising
  • Fish: the village’s livelihood and roots

Cinematography

Wide landscapes show the village’s isolation; handheld shots add raw, documentary tension. Long takes, slow transitions and backlit silhouettes give a poetic rhythm that lifts Karnan’s quiet heroism without turning it into spectacle.

The original campaign

The official posters tell a journey from oppression to uprising: many hands raising a sword for a community ready to fight, then a bruised and handcuffed Karnan whose defiant face shows resilience, then Dhanush on horseback as suffering turns into action.

Insight

Symbolism and a consistent visual tone can tell a story before the film begins. Moving from struggle to oppression to uprising gives the series an emotional arc that feels authentic and powerful.

New Storyline

Karnan, re-told

The posters

The new storyline leans on animal metaphor and symbol and reveals the themes slowly. It opens by hinting at violence through the sword and human–animal imagery, keeping an air of mystery. The title reveal introduces the rural world without giving much away. A third poster shows the protagonist with his first expression from the film, and the last places the community behind him. Layered symbolism replaces an instant hero shot.

The film

The cinematic direction would rely on slow-burn symbolic visuals. It would open on animal metaphors, natural textures and the sword to hint at violence while holding mystery. Wide, atmospheric shots would introduce the village, with the hero seen only in partial glimpses before he comes into focus. Then the frame widens to the community uniting behind him, echoing the poster series.

Fusion board

Karnan × Chittaprosad

Moodboard

Bold black-and-white line with striking red accents to set up mystery. Symbolism and gradual tension let clues unfold instead of revealing everything at once, and linocut textures and carved detail give the posters a handmade intensity that suits the film’s atmospheric, metaphor-driven style.

“Some battles are not fought for victory; they’re fought so the next child can walk without fear.”

Final Design

Title + four-poster series

Bold black-and-white line with striking red accents builds mystery. The series leans on symbolism and gradual tension, so clues unfold instead of the story arriving at once, while carved linocut texture gives each poster a raw, handmade intensity that matches the film.

Title design

The carved title

The curve across the top stands for the sword, a symbol of strength and resilience. Built in a linocut style with thick strokes and sharp-edged letters, the title feels raw, powerful and handmade.

01

Pre-title poster

The Divide

A knife splits the frame, ringed by the animal metaphors from the film. On one side the animals climb, the suppressed village reaching for growth and dignity. On the other the humans descend, the oppressive village that drags them down. The blade makes the split stark and shows the tension and violence the division creates.

02

Title poster

The Hidden Deity

The forest deity’s mask is half-hidden to keep its mystery and spiritual weight. Across the top of the deity’s crown rides the silhouette of the hero on horseback, sword in hand, introducing him and how he moves without giving too much away. The background draws the eye inward to the centre and deepens the poster’s symbolic, enigmatic tone.

03

First-look poster

The Reluctant Hero

The hero appears with a frightened face that shows his vulnerability, while a small rider at his chest stands for the courage he is trying to find for himself and his people. The black funnel behind him, a fragment of the deity’s mask, ties his journey to the film’s spiritual symbolism. It also marks the divide between the two villages, and an open door to victory and justice.

Sun, sword and horse recall the myth of Karnan, son of the sun god, and reinforce his destiny. The war scene behind him reflects the villagers’ oppression and the battles they must fight before they reach light and justice.

04

Final poster

The Protector

The hero stands firm and fearless for his village. The uneven road around him carries the long history of inequality he and his community have faced, and the black funnel returns as a fragment of the deity’s mask. Armed villagers line up behind him in solidarity. The clear road, shaped like a sword, is the path of justice he has carved for his people as protector and leader.

The Series

Pre-title, title, first look and final, read left to right: a blade, a hidden god, a frightened man and finally a protector with his people behind him.

05

Learnings

Symbols tell the story first

A consistent visual tone and a clear arc, from struggle to oppression to uprising, carried the emotion across four posters before a single frame of the film.

Mystery outlasts spectacle

Revealing the hero slowly, in partial glimpses, gave each poster a reason to wait for the next one.

A style is a point of view

Working in Chittaprosad’s linocut language meant taking on his empathy and resistance, not just his texture. The raw, grounded look is what gave the narrative its weight.

Restraint holds a series together

Black, white and a single red were enough to link four very different compositions, and they left room for the symbols to do the talking.
