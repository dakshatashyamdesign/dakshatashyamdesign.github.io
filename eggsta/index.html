Project brief

The fragile
brief

×6

Hold six eggs. Safely.

The job: a package that keeps 6 eggs safe and secure. Sounds simple, until you remember an egg can crack from a little pressure, a sudden jolt or one bad bump.

So every egg needs its own protection, while the whole pack stays lightweightcompacteasy to carry

What it had to get right

  • 1
    Shock absorptionThe number one priority.
  • 2
    A cavity per eggSeparate supports, so eggs never knock into each other in transit.
  • 3
    Strong, yet cushionedFirm enough to hold, soft enough to soak up impact.
  • 4
    Room to breatheVentilation, because eggs need airflow to stay fresh.
  • 5
    Easy everyday useOpen, close, stack and carry without hurting a single egg.
  • 6
    Less material, kinder materialMinimal use of eco-friendly options like paper pulp, cardboard or moulded fibre: light, recyclable and food-safe.

Pulp goes weak

Pressure from the top

Lids that press down on the eggs end up breaking them. The fix: diamond-shaped supports and curved edges.

Z

Eggsta talks to Gen Z and young adults who put eggs on the plate every day, and who reach for bold visuals, loud colour and playful packs.

They like branding that is quirky and full of energy, and that matches a young, always-moving lifestyle. So the carton had to feel like a mood, not just a container.

05

Learnings

Lessons,
sunny side up

01

Nature had the answer first

Watching how a hen cushions and cups her eggs gave the structure a starting point that pure guesswork never would.

02

Protection is structure, not padding

Giving each egg its own cavity, and keeping pressure off the top, mattered more than adding extra material.

03

The dieline doesn't lie

Measuring the egg first and working every panel out from it showed me how a millimetre on paper decides whether the box folds at all.

04

Even a staple can have swagger

A wordmark, a mascot and a tone of voice turned an everyday product into something a Gen Z shopper would actually notice.
