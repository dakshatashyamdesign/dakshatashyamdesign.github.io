01Project brief

Bringing back the magic of real play.

Tilly is a wonder-filled experiential toy store for kids who spend more time with screens than sunshine. Children once explored the outdoors with muddy hands and wild imaginations; today many miss those simple, sensory adventures.

Tilly recreates that feeling indoors, turning the store into safe, clean pockets of outdoor-style fun where kids can climb, build, discover and dream. The brief covered the full journey: researching how visual merchandising works in real stores, then building the brand, the space, the signage and the merchandise for a toy store of my own.

The challenge

Screens are winning.

Buttons got smaller, screens grew brighter, and the great big world outside shrank into tiny digital squares.

The idea

Outdoor magic, indoors.

A store where trees curl into walls and every corner says "come play", without the mosquitoes, mud stains or "wash your hands!" chants.

The style

Art Nouveau × Pop Art.

The graceful curves of Art Nouveau meet the colourful punch of Pop Art in a magical, modern space that sparks joy.

02Research

Mall surveySite visit · Mall of Asia

Before designing anything, I studied how stores use visual merchandising in practice. I looked at brand consistency and space planning: whether colour, type and graphics matched the brand, how people entered, paused and left, and how visibility, lighting, materials and practical limits shaped the displays. I read every store through six key elements.

01

Focal point

A striking display, built through size, colour, light or arrangement, catches the eye first and organises how shoppers move.

02

Lighting

Light reveals colour and texture, sets the mood and quietly steers people from zone to zone.

03

Space

Uncluttered layouts help shoppers focus, move with ease and stay longer, while leaving room for seasonal change.

04

Colour

Warm colours create excitement and cool colours calm. Colour also groups products and guides the eye.

05

Signage & graphics

Clear signs help people find their way, and graphics add mood and story to the displays.

06

Brand identity

When displays, light, signs and graphics all reflect one personality, the store becomes recognisable and builds trust.

Competitive analysisThree experiential toy brands

Next I compared three toy brands that already sell an experience, not just products. For each one I looked at positioning, store layout, signage and display strategy.

Toys"R"Us

One of the world's oldest toy retailers, positioned as the trusted, ultimate destination for toys and play.

Layout
Wide aisles zoned by age and toy type, with "experience zones" where kids touch, test and play.

02

Brand story

Once upon a not-so-long-ago time, kids ruled the outdoors. They chased butterflies, collected shiny pebbles, built mud castles and came home with pockets full of secrets and shoes full of sand.

Then Tilly popped into the story, bright, curious and determined. Inside Tilly, kids can climb, dig, imagine and explore, just like the good old days but safer, cleaner and bursting with creativity. Tilly isn't just a store. It's the hero of a new playtime adventure, bringing childhood wonder back to life one giggle at a time.

What if we could bring all that outdoor magic back… but without the mosquitoes, mud stains, or "Wash your hands!" chants?Tilly, wondering

Mission

To bring back hands-on, heart-filled play with immersive indoor worlds where children explore, imagine and discover, just like outdoor adventures but safer, cleaner and full of creativity.

Message

Play is meant to be felt, touched, explored and lived. When screens steal the spotlight, Tilly's storybook spaces invite kids to move, dream, create and be wonderfully, wildly curious again.

Promise

A safe, clean, delightfully immersive world for outdoor-style play without the mess, mosquitoes or worries. Every visit brings wonder, hands-on fun, and creativity blended with care.

03

Wordmark

Inspired by the Pilowlava typeface, the Tilly logo is custom lettering of soft, organic curves and droplet-like shapes: alive and storybook-like, with Art Nouveau grace and a Pop Art bounce.

The letters break typographic rules on purpose. They stretch, shrink and curve like characters with personalities of their own, so the mark is bold yet gentle, and impossible to copy.

08

Mascot

Meet Willy.

Willy is Tilly's tiny bundle of wonder: a baby panda explorer with a big smile and an even bigger mushroom hat. His bright red toadstool cap is his imagination umbrella, where stories grow and ideas wiggle.

His leafy cape and wooden button charm carry the spirit of nature and remind kids how exciting the real world can be to touch, feel and explore. Endlessly curious, he's always spotting a funny frog, counting mushroom dots or inventing a silly forest dance.

"Come on, let's play!"

09

Floor plan & materials

The plan arranges the store around a central toy-store loop. Reception, billing and the café sit along the main path, the play areas spread across both floors, and staff, storage and amenities are tucked to one side.

Curves6 mm flexible ply over plywood frames and wire mesh, finished with gypsum boards and plaster.
SurfacesClay-texture coating for a soft, sculpted, breathable finish, and large-format porcelain tiles for seamless floors.
DetailsBiodegradable mycelium name boards and textured ceilings that echo the forest floor.
04Final design

A store that feels grown, not built.

The final outcome brings the identity to life at full scale: a mushroom-inspired building, an interior of themed worlds designed around merchandising, a five-sense experience, branded merchandise, and a film that walks you through it all.

Exterior

Under the mushroom cap.

The architecture borrows the shape of a mushroom cap. Flowing, continuous curves replace rigid lines, and sweeping roofs spread outward like a canopy over the glass levels below, just as a cap shelters its delicate gills. Soft light traces each curve, so the building sits in its landscape like a calm, sheltering organism.

Reception
01 · Arrival

Reception

A giant mushroom canopy shelters a curved counter built from plywood ribs and 6 mm flexible ply, skim-coated in gypsum and finished in matte clay-texture paint, with a quartz top.

Warm-white LED strips glow beneath the cap and under the counter skirting, welcoming families into the story.

02 · Point of sale

Billing counter

The counter is a clear point of sale and a visual focal point, shaped in curves and lit with warm accents. Small toys sit at children's eye and hand level, keeping them engaged in the queue and encouraging last-minute impulse buys.

03 · The heart

Toy store

Organic forms, a loop layout and bold colour turn shopping into storytelling. A central tree display anchors the room and helps with orientation, while products at child eye level invite interaction.

Ambient and accent lighting, with seamless themed zones, keep products visible without clutter. Children stay longer, impulse buys rise, and the visit becomes an experience.

Lighting

Cove lighting gives soft, indirect light that adds depth and cuts glare. Micro LED fairy lights add a warm glow, recessed micro-downlights focus attention without cluttering the ceiling, and simple dome pendants add rhythm and scale.

Materials

Large-format porcelain tiles keep floors seamless. Wave forms are built from plywood frames with wire mesh or battens, then gypsum and plaster, and finished in clay-texture paint for a soft, handcrafted, tactile surface.

5Sensory experience

Designed for all five senses.

Tilly's merchandising goes beyond what children see. Sound, scent, touch and taste each add a layer to the story of an outdoor childhood.

Sight

Toys are staged as miniature worlds, such as a "forest clearing", "mud kitchen" or "sky trail", in pastel carved shelving. Tree columns, mushroom alcoves and glowing portals lead the eye to key collections.

Sound

A soundscape of soft music, birds, leaves and water makes the store feel like an outdoor refuge. Chimes and gentle percussion respond to play, so sound becomes another thing to discover.

Smell

Gentle, zoned scents that never overwhelm: fresh citrus and green notes for active and outdoor play, softer vanilla or lavender for reading nooks and toddler zones.

Touch

A "please touch" store. Low product islands invite kids to test blocks, sensory toys and soft toys, while carved trunks, pebble seats, cork and rounded edges make every surface part of the play.

Taste

The café and events carry the story into taste, with simple, wholesome bites and flavoured waters themed to seasons of play such as "forest picnic" and "sunny beach day".

✦Merchandise

Take a little Tilly home.

The identity extends to products and packaging: Willy's spotted mushroom hat, kids' socks, caps in the signature pattern, paper bags, cups and coffee packs, all led by the bold plum "T".

05Learnings

What Tilly taught me.

Designing a store end to end changed how I think about graphic design: it doesn't stop at the page.

01

Look before you design

Walking the mall showed me how focal points, lighting, space, colour, signage and identity work together in a real store. Those six lenses shaped every decision that followed.

02

A brand is a system, not a logo

The wordmark's curves had to stretch into illustrations, a pattern, a mascot, signage and even architecture. Keeping one visual language consistent across every scale was the real challenge.

03

Design for children, and the people with them

Eye-level displays, colour-coded wayfinding and safe, clean play spaces taught me to balance a child's delight with a parent's peace of mind, and both with what the store needs to sell.

04

Experience is multi-sensory

Planning sound, scent, touch and taste, and choosing real materials such as flexible ply, clay-texture paint and mycelium, showed me that merchandising is felt as much as it is seen.
